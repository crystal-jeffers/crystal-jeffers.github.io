Service Department · Southland Industries, Northern California

Thoughtful systems that help a growing service team work as one.

Southland's Northern California Service Department is growing, and every new hire, job and customer adds another hand-off to coordinate. I design tools and guides that keep that work consistent and connected across roles and departments, so the team can stay focused on excellent execution and an outstanding customer experience.

Each project began with listening. The service manager invited me to help with start-up technician tracking, then with making field procedures easier to use, then with onboarding. Each one builds on the last.

My Focus

Each project supports NorCal's strategic focus: engaged, well-supported people; consistent workflows across offices and departments; and lasting customer relationships.

What I bring to the table

I'm an operator who builds. I do the daily coordination work, so I see exactly where time, information and follow-through slip, and I build the fix inside the tools and habits the team already has. That's what having me on a team opens up:

  • Leaders stop being the switchboard.Status, next steps and answers reach people before they have to ask, so managers spend their time on decisions, customers and growth instead of chasing updates.
  • New people contribute sooner.Role-based guides and checklists teach the work in the order it happens, without pulling an experienced teammate off the job for every question.
  • Work doesn't depend on who remembers.The process lives in the system, not in one person's head, so a vacation, a promotion or a departure doesn't stall jobs or customer commitments.
  • Change lands instead of piling up.When a platform or process shifts, the documentation and tools shift with it, so the team keeps moving through a rollout instead of relearning by word of mouth.

The result: a department that can take on more work without the coordination load growing at the same rate.

Employee Lifecycle System

Communication · Efficiency

Built, pilot pending

A complete management interface designed to automate routine steps, track case progress, and support faster turnaround for every new hire, role change and departure.

Open the guide site ↗ How it works ↗
Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Bringing on a new hire involves IT, Safety, Fleet, Facilities, Accounting and often the union hall. Each manager carried those steps in their own way, which made progress hard to see during a busy week. Role changes and departures raised the same coordination needs, with added care for confidentiality.

What I built

Every hire, role change and departure now runs the same way for every manager, with each step owned, tracked and confirmed, so nothing waits on someone remembering.

Details
  • One engine, three case typesOnboarding, role change and offboarding each start with one form and build their own checklist from a shared step library, filtered by role, location, union, vehicle and more.
  • One owner per stepEvery hand-off is split into a request step and a confirmation step, so nothing stalls between teams.
  • Automation with judgmentManagers choose whether routine emails send for them or wait as drafts. Sensitive cases, like involuntary exits, always wait for a person.
  • Docs for each audienceA guide site with separate paths for HR and managers, a timeline, an FAQ keyed to every email, and plain-language definitions.

Business impact

Supports the goal of enhancing onboarding and ongoing support as new hires join the division and learn the NorCal Way. Every hire, role change and departure follows the same steps, whichever manager owns it, which creates consistent workflows and smoother hand-offs between HR, IT, Safety, Fleet and Accounting. Because it was built from the superintendents' own New Hire Protocols, it reflects a process they already know.

Start-Up Crew Tracking & Dispatch

Communication · Access

Built, pilot pending

A shared scheduling board for the Start-Up crew that tracks every job and accounts for each technician's union holidays, so scheduling stays consistent as the crew grows.

Supervisor guide ↗
Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Start-up crew schedules and job status were tracked in a spreadsheet. Union holidays vary by local and by agreement, so checking a technician's availability meant cross-referencing by hand.

What I built

Supervisors can schedule the whole crew from one board and see a union holiday conflict before a job is booked, not after.

Details
  • Timeline and calendar viewsEvery technician's jobs, training and time off in one place, with job tracking and a start-up checklist per job.
  • Union holidays added automaticallyEach tech gets their own local's paid holidays and off days, about 90 days ahead.
  • Warn, don't blockIf a job lands on someone's holiday, the bar is flagged and the warning clears when it moves. The supervisor keeps the final call.

Business impact

Brings consistency and predictability to crew scheduling, and gives the department room to take on more work as it grows. Union holiday rules appear right where scheduling decisions are made: at launch, 132 entries for 16 union technicians, with new crew members added automatically.

Technician Job Follow-Ups

Communication · Efficiency

In use

Personalized emails that show each technician their open service jobs, grouped by what's needed to close them: notes, a return visit, parts or scheduling.

Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Open service jobs need to close quickly, and many things can hold one up: notes still to be written, a return visit to finish the work, or parts that have arrived and just need a time on the schedule. Following up job by job meant a lot of back-and-forth.

What I built

Technicians can clear a whole list of open jobs in one reply, so jobs close sooner and customer records stay current.

Details
  • One email per technicianOpen jobs grouped by status: missing notes or confirmation, return visits needed, parts, and customer scheduling.
  • A specific question on every lineEach job shows its last visit and exactly what's needed to move it forward, so a reply can be one line.
  • Built from my recordsExcel macros assemble every email from my dispatch notes and job records. I start each run, review it and send.
  • Refined with useThe first version was color-coded by status. The current version uses clean tables by category, based on how technicians replied.

Business impact

Closes the common gaps between field work and the office. Technicians answer right in the email, often job by job, so jobs close sooner and customer records stay current. Technicians have thanked me for the lists, a sign the format works for the people using it.

  • Excel VBA
  • Email automation
  • Information design
  • Iterating from feedback

PM Parts Auditing and Ordering

Efficiency · Communication

In use

Every customer's parts list standardized, then built into an automated ordering system with a technician review, so each order is checked by the person who knows the equipment before it's placed.

Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Each customer's PM parts lived in its own Word document, with vendors, quantities, part numbers and inventory notes mixed together, and every document laid out differently. Keeping them current took constant attention, and when one customer had several orders in a month, telling the orders apart took extra effort.

What I built

Any coordinator can order any customer's PM parts correctly, and the technician who knows the equipment verifies each order before money is spent.

Details
  • Every parts document rewritten to one standardI reorganized each customer's document by service type and vendor, matching how the team already worked, so any coordinator can read any list.
  • An ordering system on topAn Excel parts list by customer, type and vendor, with macros that build each order and give it its own correct PO number, even when one customer has several orders in a month.
  • A parts audit before orderingEach technician gets the upcoming parts lists for their sites to confirm or edit. Their sign-off is tracked month by month before anything is ordered.
  • Order tracking through pickupEach order's status, from sent to received to will-call pickup, is visible at a glance.

Business impact

Reflects a commitment to excellence, with no shortcuts on what goes to a customer's site. Standard lists make the work transferable, the technician who knows the equipment reviews each order, and clear PO numbers make every order easy to trace.
