Employee Lifecycle System

ArchivedCohesion · Efficiency

A complete management interface designed to automate routine steps, track case progress, and support faster turnaround for every new hire, role change, and departure.

View user guides ↗ How it works ↗
Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Bringing on a new hire involves IT, Safety, Fleet, Facilities, Accounting, and sometimes the union hall. The steps were written down, but not who owned each one across teams. People relied on memory or assumption to know who handled what, which made it hard to follow up and work as a team to make sure every new hire was fully set up on day one. Role changes and departures raised the same coordination needs, with added care for confidentiality. With Workday still months away and the workforce changing, Start-Up and Service needed something to hold the process together in the meantime.

What I built

Every team can see which steps it owns, and every step is tracked through to confirmation, so new hires are fully set up on day one without relying on memory.

Details

  • One engine, three case typesOnboarding, role change, and offboarding each start with one form and build their own checklist from a shared step library, filtered by role, location, union, vehicle, and more.
  • One owner per stepEvery hand-off is split into a request step and a confirmation step, so nothing stalls between teams.
  • Automation with judgmentManagers choose whether routine emails send for them or wait as drafts. Sensitive cases, like involuntary exits, always wait for a person.
  • Docs for each audienceA guide site with separate paths for HR and managers, a timeline, an FAQ keyed to every email, and plain-language definitions.
  • Requirements from stakeholder docs
  • Data model
  • Rules engine
  • Human-in-the-loop
  • Role-based UX
  • Documentation

Business impact

The Manager Workspace carried Start-Up and Service through a period of workforce changes, so new hires were set up on day one while the company prepared for Workday. Clear ownership turned the hand-offs between HR, IT, Safety, Fleet, and Accounting into one shared checklist that every team could see and follow up on. Now that Workday handles most of the tracking, drafting, and request emails, the system is archived. The HR side was a design exercise in how two systems can share one process without confusion, and that thinking about visibility and ownership carries into onboarding readiness today.

Start-Up Tracking & Dispatch

In UseCohesion · Access

A shared scheduling board for the Start-Up crew that tracks every job and accounts for each technician's union holidays, so scheduling stays consistent as the crew grows.

Supervisor guide ↗
Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Crew schedules and job status lived in a monthly spreadsheet. It was time-consuming to update, hard to view at a glance, difficult to search, visually overwhelming, and not viewable on mobile. It offered limited functions and awkward back-end upkeep, and union holidays, which vary by local and agreement, were all tracked by hand. Checking one technician's availability meant cross-referencing on the fly.

What I built

The spreadsheet became a live board: supervisors plan, track, and adjust the whole crew in one place, and every technician sees their own schedule from a phone.

Details

  • The whole crew at a glanceTimeline view: one row per technician and one bar per assignment, color-coded by union. Gaps, overlaps, and open capacity show up before a date is committed.
  • Coverage by dateCalendar view: filter to see who's out and when, so coverage for a holiday week or a long job is clear before it's booked.
  • Every job's progress, without a phone callJob tracker: phase, schedule status, deadline, and contacts on every job, each with its own start-up checklist, so progress is visible without asking for an update.
  • Holidays that fill themselves inEach technician's own local's paid holidays and off days are added automatically, about 90 days ahead. New crew members get theirs the moment they're added.
  • Conflicts caught at bookingIf a job lands on someone's holiday, a warning appears right on the assignment and clears when the job moves. The supervisor still makes the final call.
  • A view-only board in every pocketTechnicians open a read-only live feed on their phones that shows only their own assignments. They always see the current schedule, and the board stays accurate because only the supervisor edits it.
  • Two weeks of coverage, planned aheadBuilt with the superintendents: a labor forecasting board looks two weeks out, flags any job without a crew and any technician without work, and lets the supervisor fill the gap in one click.
  • Next week, in their inbox every FridayAn automated email every Friday afternoon lists each technician's jobs for the coming week, so Monday starts with a plan instead of a phone call.
  • Spreadsheet-to-database migration
  • Rules modeling
  • Scheduling UX
  • Exception handling
  • Mobile access
  • Automated email

Business impact

After one demo, the superintendents and service manager adopted the board on the spot, and it is now how the Start-Up crew is scheduled. Scheduling moved from manual upkeep to a board that maintains itself, so supervisor time goes to planning the work instead of updating cells. Technicians see their own schedule anytime and get next week's plan every Friday, which cuts the calls and texts that used to fill the gaps. Holiday conflicts are caught at booking instead of on the day, avoiding last-minute reschedules with customers. And the board grows with the crew: at launch, 132 holiday entries for 16 union technicians, with new crew members added automatically. The labor forecasting board, built with the superintendents, keeps every job and every technician covered two weeks into the future.

Security & Reliability Hardening

In UseAccess · Cohesion

The safeguards behind the Dispatch Hub, Start-Up SOPs, and Employee Lifecycle sites: every control mapped to a type of risk, an IT-style security review, and a costed plan for private hosting as the team grows.

View the security plan ↗
Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

The sites debuted in a private demo with the service manager and field supervisors. They loved them right away and wanted to put them into use. Before the first day of official use, I proactively worked through what an IT team would want to see: who can edit, what belongs on a public page, and what happens when something goes wrong. The goal was quality and integrity from day one, on a free plan, with a clear path for when the company is ready to fund private hosting.

What I built

I treated the sites like any internal system an IT team would review, and built each control around a type of risk instead of a checklist alone. If one control misses, another catches it.

Details

  • IntegrityEvery save is signed by the editor, checked automatically, and reversible in one click. Broken or unsafe changes never go live.
  • ConfidentialityA classification rule decides what belongs on a page and what stays on SharePoint. Screenshots use anonymized sample data, and the publish check blocks phone numbers and emails.
  • Identity and accessA company-owned organization, required two-step sign-in, edit-only rights, same-day removal, and a quarterly access review.
  • Supply chainOutside code is pinned, integrity-checked, and served from the site itself, with alerts for known vulnerabilities.
  • People and processPlain-language error messages and an editor guide, so a blocked save tells the editor exactly how to fix it.
  • Continuity and scaleOwnership sits with the company, with two owners and written guides. Capacity math and four costed hosting options show what changes as the team grows.
  • Risk assessment
  • Security controls
  • Access management
  • Change control
  • Vendor evaluation
  • Cost modeling

Business impact

19 of 23 IT security checks are met on the free plan, at $0. The other four are mapped to paid hosting, with a funding plan that compares four options. The cheapest gives company sign-in for $0, against about $3,500 a year for GitHub Enterprise at the same team size. Capacity math puts plan limits in plain terms: a 14-person team could publish about 1,000 edits a month on the free private tier.

Migration Documentation

ArchivedCohesion · Access

Documentation that carries a team through a platform change. Here, a department moving from COINS and ServiceTrade to ServiceTitan got one iterative, searchable reference: Active articles for how the work is done today, and an archive for context on older jobs.

Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Shortly after I joined, the department began migrating from COINS and ServiceTrade to ServiceTitan. For months, work ran across all three systems while processes changed month to month. Much of the know-how was tribal knowledge: it lived in experienced people's memory and traveled by word of mouth, so the answer depended on who you asked and when they'd last checked. When details didn't match, small misses carried real costs: a PO coded as material instead of subcontractor, an invoice never uploaded and billing held up, parts that arrived without the technician hearing about it, or a job tracked in the wrong system.

What I built

The team gained one reference it could trust: current steps in one place, with the history kept for context.

Details

  1. Week-one SOPsDocumented each Service Coordinator workflow in the existing systems and shared it with the Director.
  2. Living pages in the team's toolsConverted into editable OneNote pages, where the team keeps its reference material, so anyone could add to them.
  3. Kept current through the rolloutAt the Director's request, updated each process as it moved to ServiceTitan, including a phased plan for which jobs closed out in ServiceTrade and which moved over.
  4. One consolidated ServiceTitan referenceRevised and combined the SOPs shared by the Texas team into one cohesive document that's easier to search and scan.
  5. Active and ArchiveOrganized into one searchable knowledge base: Active articles show how the work is done today, and an archive keeps the COINS and ServiceTrade steps for older jobs.
  6. CloseoutAs the last ServiceTrade jobs closed, legacy steps were archived and links pointed to the ServiceTitan articles. COINS steps stayed only where exception workflows still use it. My maintenance ended with the rollout.
  • Knowledge base design
  • Change management
  • Version control (Active / Archive)
  • Content consolidation

Business impact

Replaced tribal knowledge with a reference people could trust through the whole transition: fewer check-ins and less waiting for answers, fewer tasks redone because a small detail was forgotten, and coordinators who worked with confidence while every process was changing. The Director endorsed it in week one and asked that it continue through the rollout.

PM Efficiency, Visibility, and Reporting

In UseCohesion · Efficiency

Preventive maintenance status for every audience: each technician's own list, an all-staff summary, and a management view with parts and coverage.

Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

Preventive maintenance is the work that's easiest to put off: the lowest margin and lowest priority, buried under quoted work. Visits get pushed down the road until the backlog is massive. In October 2026, some visits from August 2025 were still incomplete. That costs three ways. Revenue is lost, because past a certain point a missed visit can't be recouped. Trust breaks, when customers have to reach out themselves to get their maintenance scheduled. And risk grows, because PMs exist to catch problems early, and a missed one can become a costly failure. Lead technicians had no line of sight into which PMs had been started, stalled, or completed.

What I built

Technicians, account managers, and leadership all see which PMs are started, stalled, or completed, so visits get scheduled, finished, and billed instead of buried.

Details

  • A monthly email per technicianOnly their outstanding and upcoming PMs, with sales and backup technicians copied so everyone covering the account stays informed.
  • An all-staff summaryEvery outstanding PM by month, with the technician and its status: ready to schedule, pending customer OK, or return visit needed.
  • A management viewColor-coded by stage, with the job, technician, backup, account manager, and parts status on one line.
  • Reporting by audience
  • Excel VBA
  • Email automation
  • Information design

Business impact

PMs are where customer trust is kept or lost. With one shared view of every visit, the backlog stays visible and shrinks instead of growing. Many stalled PMs only need notes or tasking to close, and each one closed is billable work that would otherwise be left on the table. Keeping visits on schedule protects recurring maintenance revenue, keeps customers from having to chase us, and catches equipment problems while they're still small.

Technician Follow-Ups

In UseCohesion · Efficiency

Personalized emails that show each technician their open service jobs, grouped by what's needed to close them: notes, a return visit, parts, or scheduling.

Challenge, build & impactClose

The challenge

With 200 open jobs, follow-up was grouped by hand where possible. Because it was all manual, technicians inevitably heard from dispatch several times, with different orders or follow-up questions each time, and every round trip held up closing the job.

What I built

Every open question for a technician goes out in one message with one click, so technicians answer once and jobs move to billing sooner.

Details

  • One email per technicianOpen jobs grouped by status: missing notes or confirmation, return visits needed, parts, and customer scheduling.
  • A specific question on every lineEach job shows its last visit and exactly what's needed to move it forward, so a reply can be one line.
  • One click from note to sendDispatcher notes and clarification requests are linked to each job. Once a job has a note, a status, and an assigned technician, one click groups everything for that technician and sends it. No more manual consolidating.
  • Refined with useThe first version was color-coded by status. I realized the colors helped me more than the technicians, so the current version breaks each list into separate tables by category: cleaner and easier to scan, especially for long lists.
  • Excel VBA
  • Email automation
  • Information design
  • Iterating from feedback

Business impact

Closing jobs is how service work becomes revenue. Consolidated follow-up shortens the time between finished work and a billable job, so more jobs reach billing, revenue comes in sooner, and the department stays on pace with its revenue goals. Technicians spend less time on scattered requests and more on billable work, customer records stay current, and a 200-job backlog becomes a list that moves. Technicians have thanked me for the lists, a sign the format works for the people using it.
