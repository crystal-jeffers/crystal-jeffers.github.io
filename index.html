Employee Lifecycle System

Built, pilot pending

Communication · Efficiency

Managers asked for a checklist. I delivered the checklist, and the system behind it that sends, tracks and confirms each item.

Problem, build & business impact

Problem

Onboarding lived in inboxes and memory. One new hire touched IT, Safety, Fleet, Facilities, Accounting and the union hall, and every manager did it a little differently. Steps were missed, and nobody could see where a hire stood. Role changes and departures had the same problem, plus confidentiality risk.

What I built

  • One engine, three case typesOnboarding, role change and offboarding each start with one form and build their own checklist from a shared step library, filtered by role, location, union, vehicle and more.
  • One owner per stepEvery hand-off is split into a request step and a confirmation step, so nothing stalls between teams.
  • Automation with judgmentManagers choose whether routine emails send for them or wait as drafts. Sensitive cases, like involuntary exits, always wait for a person.
  • Docs for each audienceA guide site with separate paths for HR and managers, a timeline, an FAQ keyed to every email, and plain-language definitions.

Business impact

Every new hire, promotion and departure runs through the same checklist, no matter which manager owns it, so the process holds up as the department hires more. It was built from the superintendents' own New Hire Protocols doc, matched item by item, so adoption starts from a process they already recognize.

  • Requirements from stakeholder docs
  • Data model
  • Rules engine
  • Human-in-the-loop
  • Role-based UX

Start-Up Crew Tracking & Dispatch

Built, pilot pending

Communication · Access

The service manager's team tracked start-up technicians in an Excel sheet. I gave them a scheduling board that knows each technician's union holidays.

Problem, build & business impact

Problem

Crew scheduling and job tracking lived in a spreadsheet and email. Union holidays differ by local and by agreement, so it was easy to book someone on a day they're off, and nobody would know until the day came.

What I built

  • Timeline and calendar viewsEvery technician's jobs, training and time off in one place, with job tracking and a start-up checklist per job.
  • Union holidays added automaticallyEach tech gets their own local's paid holidays and off days, about 90 days ahead.
  • Warn, don't blockIf a job lands on someone's holiday, the bar is flagged and the warning clears when it moves. The supervisor keeps the final call.

Business impact

Holiday rules that lived in union agreements now show up where scheduling decisions are made, which cuts the risk of booking a crew member on a day they're off. At launch, the board pre-loaded 132 holiday entries for 16 union technicians, and new crew members get theirs automatically.

  • Spreadsheet-to-database migration
  • Rules modeling
  • Scheduling UX
  • Exception handling

Start-Up Procedures Site

Live

Access

A 12-page procedures document nobody read, rebuilt as a phone-first site with a focused view for each role.

Problem, build & business impact

Problem

Procedures lived in one long document written for everyone at once. A technician on a job site, on a phone, had to dig for the few steps that applied to them, so mostly they didn't.

What I built

  • Role-based viewsApprentices, technicians, lead technicians and supervisors each see their own procedures first.
  • Built to be scanned on siteLarge text that respects phone settings, collapsible sections, jump-to navigation and chunked checklists.
  • Maintainable without a developerProcedures are plain text files, and one settings file controls which roles see what.

Business impact

Field staff can find the steps for their role in seconds, on the device they already carry. New hires get the same procedures from day one, and the team can keep them current without outside help.

  • Content design
  • Mobile-first

Technician PM Reporting

In use

Communication · Efficiency

Each technician gets a monthly email with only their own preventive maintenance work, sorted into what needs doing next.

Problem, build & business impact

Problem

A technician's preventive maintenance (PM) work was buried in the department's full schedule. Follow-up calls started with finding the right rows before anyone could talk about the work.

What I built

  • A monthly schedule email per technicianOnly their outstanding and upcoming PMs. Sales and backup technicians are copied, so coverage is never a surprise.
  • A personalized action emailOpen items broken into tables by what they need: notes needed, return visit needed, or ready to schedule.

Business impact

Technicians see their own to-do list at a glance, and sales and backup techs stay informed without extra emails. Phone check-ins go straight to decisions instead of searching.

  • Personalized reporting
  • Email automation
  • Information design

PM Parts Ordering with Technician Review

In use

Efficiency · Communication

An automated parts-ordering process with a quality check built in: the technician reviews the list before anything is ordered.

Problem, build & business impact

Problem

PM parts lists had constant versioning issues, so orders could go out from an outdated list, and part orders were hard to tell apart.

What I built

  • Automatic ordering with Excel macrosEvery order gets its own correct PO number, even when one customer has several orders in a month.
  • A review step before orderingEach technician gets the upcoming parts lists for their sites to check and update before the order goes out.

Business impact

The person who knows the equipment checks the list before money is spent, which means fewer wrong parts, fewer returns and fewer repeat trips. Clean PO numbers make each order easy to trace.

  • VBA / Excel macros
  • Quality control design
  • Human-in-the-loop

How I work

  • Start from the real ask.I build from the documents and habits people already have, so the new system feels like their process, not mine.
  • Give every step one owner.Hand-offs are where work gets lost. Each step has one person responsible and a clear "done."
  • Keep people in the loop where it counts.Automate the routine, and have the right person review anything sensitive or costly before it goes out.
  • Build for the next hire.Every system ships with guides written for the people who use it, so it keeps working as the team grows.
