Employee Lifecycle System

Built, pilot pending

Communication · Efficiency

They wanted a refresh. I delivered an entire management system designed to send, track and confirm each item.

Problem
Onboarding is often managed by memory and documents with no versioning control. One new hire touches IT, Safety, Fleet, Facilities, Accounting and the union hall, and managers have to track it all by hand. This is where steps get missed because there's no clear line of sight to what's done, what's still in motion, and what's stalled. The result: a stumble right at the starting line. Scrambling, last minute realizations, the works.
What I built
    One engine, three case types. Onboarding, role change, and offboarding each start with one form and build their own checklist from a shared step library, filtered by role, location, union, vehicle and more.
  • One owner per step. Every hand-off is split into a request step and a confirmation step, so nothing stalls between teams.
  • Automation with judgment. Managers choose whether routine emails send automatically or just auto-draft. (Sensitive cases, like involuntary exits, always operate on HITL.)
  • Docs for each audience. A guide site with separate paths for HR and managers, a timeline, an FAQ keyed to every email, and plain-language definitions.
Why it matters
This was built from the superintendents' own New Hire Protocols doc, matched item by item, so the people who asked for it recognize their process in it. Every new hire the department adds runs through the same steps.
  • Requirements from stakeholder docs
  • Data model
  • Rules engine
  • Human-in-the-loop
  • Role-based UX
  • Documentation

Start-Up Crew Tracking & Dispatch

Built, pilot pending

Communication · Access

The service manager's team tracked start-up technicians in an Excel sheet. I gave them a scheduling board that knows each technician's union holidays.

Problem
Crew scheduling and job tracking lived in a spreadsheet and email. Union holidays differ by local and by agreement, so it was easy to book someone on a day they're off, and nobody would know until the day came.
What I built
  • Timeline and calendar views of every technician's jobs, training and time off, with job tracking and a start-up checklist per job.
  • Union holidays added automatically. Each tech gets their own local's paid holidays and off days, about 90 days ahead.
  • Warn, don't block. If a job lands on someone's holiday, the bar is flagged and the warning clears when it moves. The supervisor keeps the final call.
Why it matters
Holiday rules that lived in union agreements now show up where scheduling decisions are made. At launch, the board pre-loaded 132 holiday entries for 16 union technicians.
  • Spreadsheet-to-database migration
  • Rules modeling
  • Scheduling UX
  • Exception handling

Start-Up Procedures Site

Live

Access

A 12-page procedures document nobody read, rebuilt as a phone-first site with a focused view for each role.

Problem
Procedures lived in one long document written for everyone at once. A technician on a job site, on a phone, had to dig for the few steps that applied to them, so mostly they didn't.
What I built
  • Role-based views for apprentices, technicians, lead technicians and supervisors. Each sees their own procedures first.
  • Built to be scanned on site: large text that respects phone settings, collapsible sections, jump-to navigation and chunked checklists.
  • Maintainable without a developer. Procedures are plain text files, and one settings file controls which roles see what.
Why it matters
Content structured by audience and task, not by document, is easier to use in the field and easier for the team to keep current as new people join.
  • Information architecture
  • Content design
  • Mobile-first
  • Accessibility
  • Docs-as-code

Technician PM Reporting

In use

Communication · Efficiency

Each technician gets a monthly email with only their own preventive maintenance work, sorted into what needs doing next.

Problem
A technician's preventive maintenance (PM) work was buried in the department's full schedule. Follow-up calls started with finding the right rows before anyone could talk about the work.
What I built
  • A monthly schedule email per technician listing only their outstanding and upcoming PMs. Sales and backup technicians are copied, so coverage is never a surprise.
  • A personalized action email that breaks open items into tables by what they need: notes needed, return visit needed, or ready to schedule.
Why it matters
Everyone sees the same to-do list at a glance, so phone calls go straight to decisions instead of searching.
  • Personalized reporting
  • Email automation
  • Information design

PM Parts Ordering with Technician Review

In use

Efficiency · Communication

An automated parts-ordering process with a quality check built in: the technician reviews the list before anything is ordered.

Problem
PM parts lists had constant versioning issues, so orders could go out from an outdated list, and part orders were hard to tell apart.
What I built
  • Automatic ordering with Excel macros, giving every order its own correct PO number, even when one customer has several orders in a month.
  • A review step before ordering. Each technician gets the upcoming parts lists for their sites to check and update before the order goes out.
Why it matters
The person who knows the equipment checks the list before money is spent.
  • VBA / Excel macros
  • Quality control design
  • Human-in-the-loop

How I work

  • Start from the real ask.I build from the documents and habits people already have, so the new system feels like their process, not mine.
  • Give every step one owner.Hand-offs are where work gets lost. Each step has one person responsible and a clear "done."
  • Keep people in the loop where it counts.Automate the routine, and have the right person review anything sensitive or costly before it goes out.
  • Build for the next hire.Every system ships with guides written for the people who use it, so it keeps working as the team grows.
